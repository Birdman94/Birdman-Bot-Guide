What this bot does

A Discord bot for Albion Online guilds: Calls to Arms, loot splits, regears, registration and payouts, built around your own Google Sheet.

The pages after this one: how a member uses it, how a night runs, officer setup, then every command. Commands only work inside a server where I'm set up — to use me in yours, message Birdman on Discord (thebirdman_94).

What it does by itself

Registration — A member runs /register and gets their guild or alliance role and nickname straight away; no officer is needed. Much of the bot relies on it. If an officer switches it on, a nightly check also finds who left the guild, tidies nicknames and tags, registers members whose Discord name is their in-game name, and asks the ones who haven't registered to /register in one message. Nobody gets a DM, and the nightly check never removes a role.
Signup threads — Once an officer starts a Call to Arms, its thread runs itself: members claim a numbered role from a drop-down menu (or by typing its number), and the bot keeps the standby list, start-time reminders, fill and bomb lists, no-show tracking and the recap.
Finding the deaths — For a regear request the bot finds the death on the killboard and keeps checking until it appears. After a loot split it checks for loot that never reached the chest.
Reports and posts — A weekly officer summary, weekly fame tracking, battleboard summaries, the objectives board, Siphoned Energy reminders, a welcome message for newcomers and nightly backups. The reports and posts are switched on in the settings menu and go where the officers choose.
Watching its own logs — Every day it checks that each log and post goes to a channel the right people can see, and tells the officers once when one doesn't. It never hides or moves a channel.
Members' own pages — /me shows a member their attendance, streak, sign-ups, wallet and Siphoned Energy balance. /alt links their other characters.

What waits for an officer's yes

Loot splits and pay — An officer logs a Call to Arms and the bot takes the guild's tax and splits the rest between everyone who came. Nobody is paid until an officer says so, and the bot never sends silver: it records what an officer sent. Every player has a wallet and everything is on record.
Regear — The bot prices a lost item, or a whole death from the killboard, at the cheapest market price for its quality in any city but Caerleon (or its tooltip EST if lower), and caps it by role. Members can file their own with a button; an officer approves it and marks it paid.
Taking roles away — A hard audit shows what it would change and asks before it removes a role. Officers can also register anyone who is stuck, and fix a record when Albion's lookup gets it wrong.
Changing a channel — If the Officer role can't see a log, the officers' note has a button that lets it in, after asking first. Nothing else about a channel is ever changed.
Settings — Only officers change settings, in one menu, and the things that act on their own are switched on there.
Extras — Click-an-emoji role panels, Siphoned Energy uploads, offtimers (side content), giveaways, recruiter rewards and an Excel report: officers start them.

Three ways to use it in a server

Full bot — Everything above, with that server's own separate data, settings and officers.
Register-only — Just registration: members verify their character and officers look after the roles.
CTA signups only — Just the numbered signup threads, built from the guild's own Google Sheet.

See it in action

What it is like to run a guild with the bot

These are pictures of the bot's own screens, drawn with example names and numbers. They show what your members and officers will see once the bot is in your server, and how much of the routine it does for them.

You set it up once. After that the bot posts the signups, reads your screenshots, finds deaths on the killboard, prices the gear, works out every share and keeps the lists. It stops and asks before anything that moves silver or changes a role. It never sends silver in the game: it records what your officers decided.

It reads your screenshots

Post the guild bank tab, the silver bags, the repair window and the party list. The bot reads the totals and the names off the pictures, so nobody types a loot total or ticks forty names. A member the game can't confirm yet can even register with a screenshot of their profile page. The reading runs on the bot's own computer and nothing is sent anywhere. It is a one-time optional install.

It does the lookups

Deaths come from the killboard, prices from the market, fights from Albion's battle list, and who is really in your guild from Albion itself. A member types their character name once, at /register. After that the bot knows who they are.

It asks before it acts

Loot splits, payouts, regears and role removals all show a preview first. Nothing is saved, paid or removed until an officer presses the button. If you want, the bot can handle clear-cut regears by itself, and says so on the ticket.

A CTA night, start to finish

Eight steps. The officer is needed for three of them.

  1. Post the signup/cta start with a tab of your sheet and a start time.🔒 Officer
  2. Members sign upA drop-down on the post. The roster and your sheet update as they click.🟢 Any member
  3. Reminders go outEveryone who signed up is pinged before the start. The server event is already on the Events list.🤖 The bot does this
  4. The fights are postedThe battleboard finds each fight, or the whole timer in one post.🤖 The bot does this
  5. Log the CTAPost the screenshots, press Read screenshots, check the preview, press Log it.🔒 Officer
  6. The split is worked outTax, cuts and every share, shown on a card in your loot channel.🤖 The bot does this
  7. Pay in the game, record it/pay, or Mark shares paid on the card. The bot only records it.🔒 Officer
  8. Everything closes upMembers are told. The signup closes itself once every share is paid.🤖 The bot does this

Every screen below uses made-up players and numbers. Wording, buttons and layout are the bot's own.

1 · CTA signups and builds

CTA signups and builds

A CTA signup is one post. The roles come from a tab of your Google Sheet, one numbered row per role. Members claim a role from a drop-down, the bot keeps the roster up to date, and every pick is written back to the sheet. Nobody copies names between Discord and a spreadsheet.

1Start it with one command

🎟️ Starter role or officer

Choose the composition tab of your sheet, give it a title and add a start time. The time is in UTC (Albion's clock). Add one and the bot also creates the server event and sets the reminders.

Leave ping_role out and the ping goes to @everyone.

2Members click, the bot keeps the roster

🤖 The bot does this 🟢 Any member

The post is the signup. Members pick an open role from the drop-down, or type its number in the thread. Typing 5 AltName brings an alt. An officer can sign someone up with 14 @Name.

Standby, fill and bomb lists are for everyone who isn't in a numbered role.

  1. 1The start time shows in each reader's own time zone, with a live countdown.
  2. 2The drop-down only lists roles that are still open, with the weapon underneath.
  3. 3The sheet's signup column is filled in as people click.

3Reminders and the server event, without asking

🤖 The bot does this

Everyone who signed up, or is on the fill list, is pinged before the start. Where the reminder goes and how early are settings. The server event was created when the signup opened, and moves if you change the start time.

4Builds: what to bring, for the role you took

🟢 Any member 🔒 Officer

/builds show opens the gear for a role: each piece with its picture, tier and amount, and any equal choices. Only the person who asked sees it. Roles-and-builds signups also show the weapon and gear columns in the roster.

Builds live on a BUILDS tab of your sheet. Officers make and edit them in a private editor with item search, and /builds post puts a menu in your builds channel so members open the one for their role.

5Afterwards: who didn't come, and a recap

🎟️ Starter role or officer 🤖 The bot does this

/cta close event_id:12, run in the signup thread, stops new claims, clears the sheet's signup column and works out who signed up but didn't come. Officers always see the names. The public recap shows only how many, unless you choose to name them.

A signup also closes itself once its loot split has been paid out.

2 · Logging a CTA and checking the loot

Loot splits, screenshots and loot check

Most of a loot split is numbers that are already on screen in the game. Post the screenshots where you run the split, press one button, and the bot reads the loot value, the silver bags, the repair cost and who was there. You check a preview and press Log it. The bot works out the tax, the cuts and every share.

1Post the screenshots, then start the log

🔒 Officer

In the channel or thread where you run the split, post the pictures you already took: the guild bank tab, the silver bag stack, the repair window and the party list. Then run /cta log with a name. You can leave the loot blank.

Prefer buttons? Log this CTA in the signup thread, or Log a CTA in /officer, does the same.

2Press Read screenshots

🔒 Officer

The preview opens with nothing saved. Press 📷 Read screenshots. The bot looks at the newest pictures posted in that channel in the last 48 hours (up to 6). Reading never changes anything: it shows what it found and where.

3Choose what to use

🔒 Officer 🤖 The bot does this

Each finding says which picture it came from. Tick what you trust and press Use the selected.

Names are matched to the characters your guild registered (mains and alts). A name the reader garbled is only matched when it is clearly one registered name, and it is listed for you to check. The repair cost is always marked "check this one".

  1. 1Every figure names its source picture and who posted it.
  2. 2The bank tab shows the value rounded, and the bot says so.
  3. 3Names it was not sure of are listed, never guessed.

4Check the split, then Log it

🔒 Officer

The preview now has the whole split. Add from battles can add anyone the battleboard saw who isn't on the party list. Nothing is saved until you press Log it. If the loot is still 0 when you press it, the bot warns you first, because the screenshots probably weren't read.

5One card for whoever pays

🤖 The bot does this 🔒 Officer

After Log it the bot posts a card in your loot split channel, so whoever handles the silver works from one place. Pay people in the game, then press Mark shares paid. It shows the live amounts and asks first. The bot never sends silver.

Edit this split (also /cta edit) changes anything afterwards, shows old and new before it applies, and keeps a history. A screenshot of a later bank tab can add late loot.

6Check the loot against the chest

🎟️ Starter role or officer

/lootcheck takes the Loot Logger's export and the guild chest log. It lists what players picked up that never reached the chest, split into your guild, your alliance and everyone else, and checks the killboard so loot someone died carrying isn't blamed on them. With several loggers it counts a pickup once.

An officer can open a loot thread from it to follow what is left. Officers and the logistics role can run it.

7Sell the loot tab

🔒 Officer

If your guild sells the tabs, each logged split's loot tab can be put up for sale to your tab buyers: a post, a thread named like WTS TAB LYM 85%, and a ping to the buyers' role.

The screenshot reader is an optional one-time install on the bot's computer. Without it everything still works; you type the numbers with Edit numbers.

3 · The combined battleboard

The combined battleboard

Every big fight is posted without anyone pasting a link. After a CTA timer the bot posts one breakdown of the whole night and keeps it up to date as Albion lists more fights. Underneath are pages anyone can open, privately.

1Point it at your guild once

🔒 Officer

Give the guild or alliance, the channel, and what to post: each battle on its own, the whole timer combined, or both. The minimum players, how often it checks and how strict Remove Rats is are settings.

2One post per CTA timer

🤖 The bot does this

As soon as the bot finds the first fight of a timer it posts the breakdown: each fight's start, link and player count, and dark table cards for the alliances and then the guilds. It counts only fights that pass your minimums.

The post is edited as Albion lists more fights, which can be hours late. Older posts keep working after a restart.

  1. 1Seven page buttons. Each opens privately, only for whoever pressed it.
  2. 2The regear button only offers that battle's deaths.

3Press a page. The bot says how far it has got

🤖 The bot does this

The fights are read once and shared by every button, so most pages open straight away. When a big fight takes a while, the "thinking" message turns into a bar with the fight and page it is on and about how long is left. If Albion's servers are busy it says so, asks again, and stops waiting after 3 minutes and shows what it has.

4Highlights

🟢 Any member

Highlights shows the player with the most kills, the most kill fame and the biggest fame death, with the gear they wore. The drop-down looks at another guild or at everyone.

A plain dim box is an empty slot. A letter would mean that piece's picture is missing.

5Composition, grouped by role

🟢 Any member

Every player is placed under a role: Tank, DTank, Healer, Support, DPS, Battlemount, Caller, your own roles, Other, Not seen. A weapon's role comes from your Builds sheet, then from the role past signups gave it. Witchwork counts as DPS. A player on a battle mount is counted once, under the mount's name.

Not seen lists by name the players whose gear is nowhere in the record.

6Kill timeline and momentum

🟢 Any member

The kill timeline is each side's running kill count. Momentum is your lead over everyone else, with its best and worst point and the biggest swings against you. Both are also in /battleboard report for several fights at once.

Albion lists fights hours late and its servers are sometimes busy. The post is edited when more of the timer's fights appear.

4 · The officer menus and first-time setup

Officer menus and first-time setup

A new server does not start with a wall of commands. The first-time setup is a checklist with buttons, the settings live in one menu you click through, and a private test copy lets you try everything with pretend players before anything real is touched. Once it runs, officers have one page that lists what is waiting for them.

1A checklist that reads your real settings

🔒 Officer

/help topic:First-time setup shows where you are. Open a step and change its setting right there with buttons: no commands to type, no hunting through menus. Run it again any time and it re-checks every setting.

Next thing to do jumps to the next step that still needs you. Quick start fills in the basics for a new guild and shows exactly what it will change before it saves anything.

  1. 1Progress is worked out from your server's actual settings, not from what you clicked.
  2. 2Steps that post something (the settings menu, the regear button) ask for a channel and post it for you.

2Try everything on pretend players first

🔒 Officer 🟢 Any member

The test menu is a channel with one button. Pressing it gives that person a private thread, a throwaway copy of the bot, pretend players and pretend silver. Each test runs the bot's real code and shows what it checked and what it would have posted. Hand the button to a friend or a role to let them try it too.

3One settings menu, always posted

🔒 Officer

/settings panel posts a permanent message in an officers-only channel with a button for each part of the bot. It is the one place settings are changed. Everything saves as you click, a page shows what each choice does with an example, and See everything lists what is set up and what needs attention.

Parts you don't use can be switched off under Functions, so their commands leave the / list.

4The everyday jobs, one click each

🔒 Officer

/officer posts a panel of buttons for what officers do most: start or log a CTA or offtimer, pay a member, open the inbox, pay every approved regear, pull a battle report, get a ping when a timer's fights show up, and open the settings. Every button opens privately and shows what it will do before it saves anything.

The same tools sit under a right-click on any member (Apps, then Officer tools).

5One page of everything waiting on you

🔒 Officer 🤖 The bot does this

/inbox collects what needs an officer: silver to pay, payments the bot recorded for you to check, regear requests, loot threads that still owe items and the Siphoned Energy upload. It only shows and links. Nothing is paid or approved from it until you press a button, and the weekly summary carries the same lines.

5 · Automated regears

Regears, mostly automatic

Members file their own regear with a button. The bot finds the death on the killboard, lists what was lost and prices it, and opens a thread for the officers with everything in one place. The officer approves in one click. Or, if you choose, the bot handles the clear-cut ones and tells you what it did.

1Set it up once

🔒 Officer

/regear panel posts the button in a channel and makes each request a thread, private or public. Choose who reviews requests (as many roles as you like) and, if you want, whether the bot may handle clear-cut ones. The same button also appears under battleboard posts, for that battle.

2A member presses the button

🟢 Any member

They type the character that died. With one recent death on the killboard they are done. With several they pick which. Deaths already regeared, or already in another request, are left out. A member can have 3 requests open at a time.

A thread named with the character and the date opens straight away.

3The officers get one card

🤖 The bot does this 🔒 Officer

The card shows the death, killer, kill fame and IP, what was lost, whether the death fell during a CTA or battle the member was on, their last regears and their wallet. The officer decides.

Approve at guild rate files the claim at your reimbursement percentage in one click. Approve & mark paid records that you already sent the silver. Price & approve opens the full kit screen. The bot never sends silver.

  1. 1Checked against the signup and the battle, so a death at a CTA the member skipped is flagged.
  2. 2Prices come from the market, every lost item at its own quality.

4If the death isn't on the killboard yet

🤖 The bot does this

Albion publishes deaths late. The request waits, the bot looks again after 24 hours and then every day, and pings the member and the officers when the death appears. On day 7 it asks for a screenshot and the officer can approve by hand.

5Optional: let the bot handle the clear-cut ones

🤖 The bot does this

Off by default. A request is clear-cut only when the death is on the killboard, the member is on the attendance of a CTA that clearly covers it, every lost item has a price and the amount is under your limit. Anything else goes to the officers as before.

Two modes: the bot approves and an officer pays, or the bot also records it as paid (or pays it into the wallet) and an officer checks it with Looks right or Undo.

6Pay in batches, then the ticket tidies itself

🔒 Officer 🤖 The bot does this

Approved claims show in /inbox, and one button marks them all paid after you send the silver. When a claim is paid the card turns to Paid, the member is told and the thread is archived. Optional settings delete paid threads after a while, or post a record of each finished ticket to an archive channel. The requests and claims stay in the bot either way.

Officers can still file a regear themselves: /regear kit prices a whole death from the killboard and /regear claim one item.

6 · Siphoned Energy

Siphoned Energy, without a spreadsheet

Siphoned Energy is the guild's shared stash, and the in-game log of who put in and who took out is a wall of lines. Copy it into a text file, upload it, and the bot works out everyone's balance. Nobody keeps a spreadsheet, and nobody works out the differences by hand.

1Copy the log from the game and upload it

🔒 Officer

In the game, copy the Siphoned Energy log into a .txt or .csv file. Then run /siphon upload and attach it. The bot reads the lines in English or in the columns of a spreadsheet, and tells you about any it could not read.

Upload as often as you like. Lines already counted are recognised and never counted twice.

2Everyone's balance, worked out for you

🤖 The bot does this

The bot adds up each player's deposits and withdrawals, matches names to registered members, and shows who is behind. Players with more than one character are counted together once they are linked. Anything it could not read is listed, not silently dropped. Undo this upload removes only the lines that upload added.

/siphon report gives everyone's balance and a CSV, or one player's page.

3Ping the ones who owe, after you have looked

🔒 Officer

Pinging shows you the message and exactly who it will name first. Nothing is sent until you press the button. Players who are not registered, who have left the server, or who you have left out on purpose (/siphon exempt) are listed but not pinged. The wording, the channel and how far behind counts are settings.

4The bot reminds you when it is time

🤖 The bot does this

Every 14 days, or the number of days you choose, the Siphon channel gets a reminder to upload a fresh log, so the picture stays current without anyone having to remember. If an upload is due or members are behind, /inbox says so too.

7 · Audits

Audits that check, ask and report

Keeping the member list honest is a chore most guilds do by hand: who left, who changed guild, who never registered. The bot checks against Albion, reports to the officers, and only ever changes a role after an officer has seen the list and pressed a button.

1Every night, quietly

🤖 The bot does this

Once the nightly audit is switched on, it looks at the members with your guild and ally roles. Where it can, it registers people by their Discord name when it matches a character in your guild. It asks the rest to /register, and tells officers who is still missing.

It stays polite: at most 40 look-ups a night, the same person is named at most 3 times and at least a week apart, and nobody gets a DM.

2Members are asked in the channel, not by DM

🤖 The bot does this 🟢 Any member

The message names the people it is for and says what to do. It says nothing else about them. The member runs /register, and the next night's audit sees them as done.

3Clearing out members who left: you stay in control

🔒 Officer

/registration audit compares the role-holders to Albion, optionally to the in-game roster you attach. It sorts them into confirmed no longer eligible, couldn't verify and the people it will not touch. Nothing changes until you press the red button. Members who show no guild at all are not treated as gone, since Albion can lag for new joiners; they are asked to /register instead.

8 · Member payouts

Payouts members can see

A payout day goes from a spreadsheet and a lot of scrolling to: look at one page, send the silver in the game, press one button. Members can see what they are owed whenever they like, and the bot reminds them once a week so they don't have to ask.

1The officer sees who to pay, in one place

🔒 Officer

/inbox (shown in chapter 4) lists every approved regear claim and unpaid loot share, with a total. Send the silver in the game, then record it. The bot never sends silver itself.

  1. 1/pay player: pays everything one player is owed. Add event_id to close out one event, or claim_id for one regear claim.
  2. 2Or press Mark approved regears as paid… in /inbox to do every approved claim at once.

2Members check their own balance

🟢 Any member

/balance shows a member what is owed to them, split into loot, regear and their wallet. /balances history shows what has been paid so far. /me is their own page: events attended, their last 10, their streak and their rank.

  1. 1The wallet holds offtimer credit and bonuses the officers have given. It is paid out the same way.

3Their own page, any time

🟢 Any member

/me is private to the member: their characters, attendance, streak and rank, how many sign-ups turned into showing up, and what is waiting for them. Officers can open anyone's page.

  1. 1Example data. Alts, Siphoned Energy and time off appear on the page when the member has them.

4A weekly note for anyone owed, if you switch it on

🤖 The bot does this 🟢 Any member

Off until the officers turn it on. Members who are owed silver get a private message once a week with the amount and the way to check it. It never names anyone else and can be stopped with one press.

5A summary for the officers, once a week

🤖 The bot does this 🔒 Officer

A thread called “📊 Weekly summary 9 Nov” appears on the day and hour you choose. Every section is a toggle: CTAs, offtimers, the guild fund, who is owed, what is waiting and member counts.

9 · New members

From joining to registered

A stranger joins your Discord because they saw it somewhere. The bot greets them, points them to the right place, and links them to their Albion character the moment they type their name. The officers don't copy names between lists.

1A welcome that points the right way

🤖 The bot does this

The bot welcomes each new member in your welcome channel. It says where to apply and how to register, using your own channel. Replace the text with your own if you like.

2They type their name; the bot checks Albion

🟢 Any member 🤖 The bot does this

/register asks for the character name. The bot looks the character up on Albion, checks the guild, then gives the role, sets the nickname and writes the rows in your sheets. The member sees what was done. Officers see nothing to copy.

  1. 1Allies and other servers can use their own roles.
  2. 2Anything that needs a person's attention shows under ⚠ Needs attention rather than failing quietly.

3If Albion is down, the bot keeps trying for them

🤖 The bot does this 🟢 Any member

Albion's API goes down, and it is slow to learn about new joiners. Instead of an error, the member is told the bot is trying. It checks once a minute for the first quarter hour, then every couple of hours for up to 48 hours, and finishes the registration when it can, with a note in the channel.

If you allow it, a screenshot of their profile page registers them provisionally in the meantime, so they are not waiting.

4Officers can always step in

🔒 Officer

Right-click any member, then Apps, then Officer tools, to open their page. Register links them to a character for them, exactly as /register would. Nobody is stuck if they cannot run the command. The audits (chapter 7) pick up anyone who never registers and ask them politely.

👤 For members

The few things most members ever do.

Get set up

  • /register — prove which Albion character is yours. You get your guild role and nickname.
  • /alt — link a second character. It can sign up too, and still counts as you. If your guild pays alt shares, each of your characters that was in the battle earns you its own loot share.
  • Registering matters: it's how the bot knows which Albion character is yours, so you're found when attendance is imported and your alts and page work. Stuck? An officer can register you. Until you do, the bot reminds you now and then (at most once a day) when you sign up or use a command.

Sign up for a CTA

  • On the signup post, pick your role from the Pick your role menu. Leave gives it back and Withdraw takes you off everything. Prefer typing? Type a role's number in the thread, e.g. 5 (-5 gives it back).
  • Every role taken? Press Standby (or type s): you get pinged when one opens, and you claim it from the menu or by typing its number. Press it again (or type -s) to leave.
  • Taking whatever's left? Press Fill: anything, or Fill: choose roles to pick the kinds you'd take (or type fill for anything), then make sure to claim a role for attendance and loot split; your caller may place you on an open one, and you're pinged at the start if one is still open. In a separate bomb squad that shares the loot split? Press Bomb (or type bomb). Press the button again (or type -bomb, -fill) to leave.
